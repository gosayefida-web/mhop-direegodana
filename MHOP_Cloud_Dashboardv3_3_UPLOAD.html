MHOP Protocol reference
Clinical pathway across the glycaemic continuum
Stage A — Prediabetes / NDH (HbA1c 42–47 mmol/mol)
Entry criteriaHbA1c 42–47 mmol/mol · preserved insulin secretion · no prior diabetes diagnosis
TIR target≥ 70%
Primary goalRemission (HbA1c < 42) or stabilisation
Escalation triggerHbA1c exceeds 48 mmol/mol or progressive deterioration despite optimisation
Stage B — Established T2DM (HbA1c > 48 mmol/mol)
Entry criteriaConfirmed T2DM · progressive insulin resistance · declining β-cell reserve
TIR target≥ 70%
Primary goalControlled T2DM · prevent multisystem deterioration
Escalation triggerPersistent poor control · declining renal / BP / weight / foot health · advanced β-cell failure
Stage C — Advanced T2DM with β-cell failure
Entry criteriaLong-standing T2DM · severe β-cell failure · insulin dependence · high disease burden
Key markersC-peptide · HOMA-β · glycaemic variability · insulin requirements
Primary goalRestoration of endogenous insulin secretion · reduced insulin dependence · stabilisation
Advanced therapyCell-based and regenerative approaches after exhaustion of conventional strategies
CGM targets — international consensus

Cardiovascular risk assessment
The dashboard computes a 10-year cardiovascular risk band for every patient with sufficient data, using two calculators side by side. The two calculators use the same six inputs — age, sex, smoking status, systolic blood pressure, total cholesterol, and diabetes status — and produce a risk band on the same five-band scale, but they reach their conclusion through different methodologies. When they agree, the patient's risk is unambiguous. When they disagree, the clinician sees both bands and exercises judgement.
Calculator 1WHO 2019 · Eastern Sub-Saharan Africa regional chart
Calculator 2Globorisk · Ethiopia country-specific calibration
InputsAge, sex, smoking, systolic BP, total cholesterol, diabetes status
Output bands< 10% · 10–<20% · 20–<30% · 30–<40% · ≥ 40%
Calibration rangePatients aged 40–74; under-40s show "Below age threshold"
Prediabetes phenotyping — OGTT
Prediabetes is not a single condition. Patients with the same HbA1c can have different underlying physiology, and the difference matters for what intervention is likely to work for them. The Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) — fasting plasma glucose and a 2-hour value after a 75 g oral glucose load — distinguishes three impaired-glucose-regulation phenotypes. MHOP performs the OGTT at the baseline clinical assessment for every enrolled patient who is not already confirmed as having T2DM.
IFGIsolated impaired fasting glucose · fasting 6.1–6.9 mmol/L · 2h < 7.8
IGTIsolated impaired glucose tolerance · fasting < 6.1 · 2h 7.8–11.0
IFG + IGTCombined impairment · fasting 6.1–6.9 · 2h 7.8–11.0 · highest progression risk
CriteriaWHO 1999/2006 (fasting threshold 6.1 mmol/L)
Not done inConfirmed T2DM patients at baseline (Stage B and Stage C)
Phenotype versus glycaemic state — the analytical design
The dashboard distinguishes two concepts that are easy to confuse: the patient's phenotype and the patient's current glycaemic state. They are tracked separately because they answer different questions, and the relationship between them is the principal analytical structure of the MHOP observational design.
The phenotype is a permanent attribute of the patient, analogous to sex and year of birth. It is one of IFG, IGT, or IFG + IGT, and it is assigned at the date of the first OGTT to show impairment. Once assigned, it does not change. Normal Glucose State (NGS) is not a phenotype — it describes the absence of impairment and leaves the phenotype field unset.
The current glycaemic state moves freely as the patient's physiology responds to intervention or progresses. It takes values NGS, IFG, IGT, IFG + IGT, or T2DM, derived from the most recent available data (most recent OGTT, or HbA1c if no recent OGTT exists).
The relationship between them tells the clinical story. A patient with the IFG phenotype whose interventions succeed reaches a glycaemic state of NGS — the phenotype remains IFG because IFG was their phenotype at the moment of determination. A patient with the IGT phenotype who progresses despite intervention reaches a glycaemic state of T2DM — the phenotype remains IGT. The cohort statement "of x patients with the IFG phenotype, y regressed to NGS and z progressed to T2DM" is the kind of analytical output the design produces.
